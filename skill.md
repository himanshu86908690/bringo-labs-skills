# Website Content Strategy

Build page content around the visitor's situation, questions, and desired outcome so they understand the business quickly and can take a useful next step. Treat clarity and intent satisfaction as the goals; do not promise conversion improvements without evidence.

Use this editorial model throughout: **Intent satisfaction = Relevance + Answer speed + Clarity + Completeness + Proof + Low friction.** This is a qualitative framework, not a numerical formula or a substitute for the 85% discovery gate. Completeness means enough information to complete this visitor's task, not everything the company could say.

Aim for this visitor progression: **"Yes, this is for me -> They understand my problem -> I understand their solution -> I trust it -> I know what to do next."** Adapt it to the page's actual intent; do not turn it into five mandatory sections.

## Discover before drafting

1. Identify the requested page, business, target audience, and scope. For multiple pages, collect the shared business brief once, then assess readiness separately for each page. Do not assume every page has the same intent or action.
2. Extract answers already present in the conversation and supplied materials. Separate confirmed facts, source-backed observations, tentative interpretations, and unknowns. Do not ask questions already answered.
3. Read [discovery-and-page-types.md](references/discovery-and-page-types.md) for question prompts and the relevant page type. Ask the most consequential unanswered questions first, generally 4-6 related questions per round. Offer short examples or choices when the user is unsure; do not overwhelm them with the entire bank.
4. Reflect back the emerging audience and intent in plain language. Resolve contradictions with targeted follow-ups. A buyer and end user may differ; establish whose decision this page supports.
5. Apply the readiness gate below after each round. While below it, provide only the evolving brief, the readiness estimate, and the next questions. Do not draft headlines, page copy, or a proposed section structure yet. Continue discovery when the user answers; silence is not an answer.

When reviewing existing copy, you may identify issues before the gate, but withhold a rewrite until ready. The gate applies to creating or changing page messaging, not to unrelated tasks or requests to explain this skill. If the user explicitly overrides the gate for a provisional draft, follow that instruction, state the gaps, and do not describe it as validated or ready to publish.

## Readiness gate: target 85%

Use this as a transparent completeness heuristic, not a scientifically measured confidence score. For each row assign 0 (missing or contradictory), 0.5 (partially usable), or 1 (specific enough to guide copy), then multiply by its weight and sum. Explain partial scores using the missing information. Do not increase scores just to start writing.

| Brief area | Weight | What must be understood |
|---|---:|---|
| Primary audience and situation | 15 | Who this is for, their trigger/problem, awareness, and relevant eligibility |
| Offer and business | 15 | What is offered, what is included, how it works, and important limits |
| Visitor intent and first question | 15 | Why they arrived, what they need first, and their principal task |
| Primary action and next step | 15 | Desired action, destination or mechanism, commitment, and what happens afterward |
| Outcomes and differentiation | 10 | Benefits that matter and credible reasons to choose this offer |
| Objections and decision information | 10 | Main friction, price/terms approach, risk, fit, and reassurance needed |
| Available proof | 5 | Which evidence can be used, its source, or confirmation that proof is unavailable |
| Customer language and tone | 5 | Their vocabulary, preferred language, tone, and terms to avoid |
| Arrival and page context | 5 | Page type, traffic source or unknown status, campaign/search context, and device priorities |
| Constraints and success | 5 | Content boundaries, brand/legal constraints if relevant, and what success means |

Begin drafting only at 85/100 or higher AND with the first four areas fully resolved. User statements or reliable supplied materials can resolve facts; speculative assumptions cannot. Confirm consequential audience, intent, and action interpretations with the user when sources are ambiguous. A CTA destination need not be a live URL if its mechanism and expected next step are clear.

An explicit absence can resolve a field: for example, "we have no testimonials" permits an evidence-safe approach. "I don't know" alone is not a full answer when the gap changes the message. Suggest ways to resolve it, such as choosing a primary audience or removing an unsupported claim. Do not require the user to invent differentiators or provide sensitive customer data.

At the gate show a concise brief, the score, and any remaining non-blocking gaps. Then proceed without a separate approval round. Explain any operational assumptions; omit unsupported factual claims.

## Map visitor intent

Answer these explicitly in the brief:

1. Why did the visitor land here? What event, search, referral, ad, or need brought them?
2. What question do they want answered first?
3. What information are they looking for to complete their task or decide?
4. What could prevent them from converting or completing that task?
5. Is search intent informational, commercial, transactional, or local? Select a primary intent and meaningful secondary intent, if any. If search is irrelevant, say so and map arrival intent instead. Local intent may coexist with commercial or transactional intent.

Also name the visitor's exact task: learn, compare, buy, book, verify, or contact. A broad search-intent category alone does not establish what the page must help them do.

Translate the map into a content decision: answer-first guidance for informational visitors; fit, differences, and evidence for comparison; price, terms, and action details for ready buyers; availability, area served, and practical contact details for local visitors. Do not force lead generation onto a page whose useful outcome is answering a question.

If traffic or search evidence is unavailable, label the intent assessment as a hypothesis and confirm it with the user. Use authorized research when needed; do not imply that search results, customer interviews, or analytics were inspected when they were not.

## Build the message hierarchy

Create a short ordered outline after the gate. Each section must answer a real visitor question, reduce a decision barrier, or support the next action. Use the visitor's decision order rather than a fixed marketing template.

- Lead with the answer visitors need first. Make the offering and audience recognizable before introducing brand slogans.
- Put the most consequential benefits, fit criteria, price information when decisive, and proof near the claims or decisions they support.
- Explain the mechanism or process enough to make the promise believable. Translate features into practical outcomes without inventing results.
- Address the strongest objections before the action that requires confidence. Put essential terms near that action rather than hiding them in FAQs.
- Use headings that communicate information when skimmed. Avoid repetitive benefit sections, long introductions, and generic headings that say nothing about the offer.
- Use FAQs only for remaining genuine questions; do not use them as a dumping ground for the core proposition.

For each section, briefly record: visitor question, core message, available proof, and action if needed. Use only the sections necessary for this page.

## Pass the five-second clarity test

The above-the-fold copy and content placement notes must quickly answer:

- What is this?
- Who is it for?
- What result does it provide?
- Why should I care?
- What should I do next?

Usually this takes a concrete headline, a short explanatory subheading, a clearly named primary action, and relevant reassurance or evidence when available. Do not force all five answers into the headline or fabricate a proof strip. An informational page can lead with a direct answer and a useful next step.

Review the hero in isolation. Map each answer to actual words; rewrite vague or missing answers. This is an editorial check, not a claim that real visitors passed a timed test. If a rendered page is unavailable, state that visibility above the fold, especially on mobile, still needs layout verification.

## Write in customer language

Use customer phrases from supplied reviews, interviews, support questions, sales notes, or the user's description. Distinguish verbatim quotations from paraphrases and suggested wording. Never invent customer quotes.

Prefer concrete nouns, active verbs, short paragraphs, and familiar terms. Define technical terms when this audience needs them; retain precise specialist language when the audience uses it. Remove buzzwords, inflated superlatives, unexplained acronyms, and vague promises such as "unlock your potential" or "seamless solutions." Explain what actually changes for the customer.

Keep outcomes proportionate to evidence. Do not fabricate statistics, client logos, testimonials, certifications, guarantees, prices, availability, urgency, or superiority claims. If evidence is missing, omit the claim, qualify it honestly, or identify the evidence needed outside the copy. Clearly separate internal notes from publishable text.

## Make the conversion path clear

Choose one primary action per page. Repeat it where helpful using consistent wording and destination; add a secondary action only for a distinct readiness level or necessary task, with lower emphasis.

Use specific labels such as "View plans," "Book a consultation," or "Check availability" when accurate. Explain what happens next, what information is needed, and known cost or commitment. Do not invent response times or describe something as free when that is unconfirmed.

Match the CTA to intent and traffic promise. Keep crucial price conditions, eligibility, service area, delivery limits, or cancellation terms close to the relevant decision. Recommend only necessary form fields. Do not use false scarcity, concealed terms, guilt, or other misleading pressure.

Where appropriate, supply concise form labels, helper text, error guidance, and confirmation text consistent with the actual process. Do not invent functioning interactions, bookings, or integrations.

## Deliver and review

Read [content-quality-review.md](references/content-quality-review.md) when planning the hierarchy, reviewing existing content, or checking a draft. Apply its 12 positive practices and 12 failure patterns to the requested scope. Fix relevant failures before delivering copy; when a fix needs missing business facts, ask targeted questions instead of inventing details. Preserve the discovery gate for rewrites.

Review the six intent-satisfaction dimensions separately as **met**, **needs work**, or **not assessable**, with a specific passage or missing fact supporting each judgment. For narrow edits, review only the affected dimensions and label the limited scope. Do not average away an unclear offer, mismatched intent, unsupported claim, hidden decision-critical detail, or unexplained next step. Summarize material findings in the final quality check rather than attaching a repetitive checklist to every delivery.

Default deliverable, scaled to the requested scope:

1. A concise audience/intent brief with readiness and remaining gaps.
2. The message hierarchy and short reasons for the order.
3. Finished page copy in section order, with headlines, body text, CTA labels, and necessary microcopy. Keep content and implementation notes separate. Avoid a pile of alternatives unless requested or a specific uncertainty warrants them.
4. Relevant supporting content only: for an indexable page, suggest a unique title and accurate meta description when useful; for visuals, suggest what they should demonstrate and meaningful alternative text only when the image is known. Do not keyword-stuff or promise rankings.
5. A compact quality check: the five hero answers, intent coverage, primary action/next step, resolved objections, claim support, and remaining factual or layout checks. Surface genuine blockers without presenting unfinished copy as publication-ready.

For narrow edits, return the requested change and the relevant checks rather than an entire strategy document. For existing pages, preserve verified details and user-specified structure unless changing them is within scope. Do not publish, redesign, or implement the site merely because content was requested.

If measurement is requested, propose a meaningful outcome such as completed qualified enquiries, purchases, bookings, or successful information finding. Match the hypothesis to the observed uncertainty. Label proposed tests as proposals; do not report imagined conversion lifts or test results.
