# What makes page content satisfy intent

Use these criteria to make editorial decisions, not to add sections mechanically. Judge content against the confirmed audience, visit intent, page type, and available evidence.

## 12 practices to build into the content

1. **Match the exact search or visit intent.** Identify whether the visitor wants to learn, compare, buy, book, verify, or contact, and help them accomplish that specific task.
2. **Give the main answer immediately.** Put the answer or offer where the visitor first needs it. Do not delay basic understanding until several sections down.
3. **Make the business understandable in five seconds.** State what it does, who it serves, and the outcome it provides. Also check why the visitor should care and what to do next, as required by the main skill.
4. **Structure around user questions.** Answer "Can they solve my problem?", "How does it work?", "What will it cost?", and "Can I trust them?" where relevant. Company information should help answer a visitor question.
5. **Prioritize by importance.** Present the essential answer, then supporting information, then details. Avoid chronological company storytelling unless that is the visitor's actual task.
6. **Be specific.** Replace "We deliver innovative solutions" with a factual description such as "We design and build conversion-focused Shopify stores for D2C brands" only if it accurately describes the business. Define D2C or use "direct-to-consumer brands" if the audience needs it. Describing a focus is not evidence of improved conversion results.
7. **Show outcomes alongside features or services.** Explain what changes for the customer, how the offering contributes, and any relevant limits.
8. **Reduce uncertainty throughout.** Explain the decision-relevant process, timeline, deliverables, pricing expectations, requirements, revisions, and support. Include only applicable, confirmed details.
9. **Place proof next to important claims.** Use relevant case studies, project examples, sourced numbers, approved testimonials, screenshots, credentials, clients, or methodology. Match the strength of the claim to what the evidence actually establishes; methodology alone does not prove a performance outcome.
10. **Make content easy to scan.** Use informative headings, short paragraphs, bullets for parallel details, meaningful labels, and restrained emphasis on critical information. A headings-only skim should communicate the main story.
11. **Answer objections before asking for commitment.** Address the relevant concerns about price, trust, experience, complexity, risk, time, and alternatives where they affect the decision.
12. **Give a clear next step.** Choose the appropriate action, such as book a consultation, view projects, get a quote, start a trial, or compare plans. These are alternatives, not a mandatory funnel. Match the action to readiness and explain what follows.

## 12 failure patterns and their remedies

| Failure pattern | What to change |
|---|---|
| 1. Generic marketing language | Replace "best-in-class," "cutting-edge," "innovative," and "world-class solutions" with concrete facts about the offer, audience, or outcome. |
| 2. Company before customer | Move long "We are passionate..." introductions behind the visitor's problem and answer, or remove them if they add no decision value. |
| 3. Unclear hero | Replace clever but ambiguous headlines with recognizable business, audience, and outcome language. |
| 4. Too much information before the answer | Move the main answer up; keep explanation available after it. |
| 5. Keyword stuffing | Use natural customer language and accurate terminology; remove forced repetition and irrelevant search phrases. |
| 6. Features without benefits | Connect each important capability to a useful customer outcome. Remove low-value lists rather than inventing benefits. |
| 7. Unsupported claims | Substantiate, qualify, or remove claims such as "#1," "trusted," "high converting," or "industry-leading." Do not replace one unsupported claim with another. |
| 8. Repeated content | Merge sections that repeat the same promise. Keep repetition only when it serves orientation or a useful repeated action. |
| 9. Huge paragraphs | Split by question or idea, add meaningful headings, and use bullets where they improve scanning. Do not fragment connected thoughts arbitrarily. |
| 10. Trying to address every audience | Prioritize the confirmed audience and task. When a homepage genuinely serves multiple groups, provide clear routes instead of blending every promise into vague copy. |
| 11. Hidden decision-critical information | Surface relevant pricing indications, timelines, process, deliverables, eligibility, and expectations. If a fact is unknown, flag the gap; do not invent it or conceal it behind a generic CTA. |
| 12. CTA before sufficient confidence | Explain the value and resolve relevant concerns before demanding commitment. Do not repeat "Book a Call" as a substitute for information. An early CTA can still serve ready visitors when the offer and next step are understandable; other visitors need a useful path to supporting details. |

## Six-dimension review

**Intent satisfaction = Relevance + Answer speed + Clarity + Completeness + Proof + Low friction.** Review each dimension independently; this is an editorial model, not measured user behavior or an arithmetic score.

| Dimension | Review question | Evidence to examine |
|---|---|---|
| Relevance | Does this serve the specific audience, arrival promise, and visit task? | Hero, terminology, offer, and proposed action compared with the confirmed brief |
| Answer speed | Can visitors find their main answer immediately? | First meaningful content and location of decision-critical answers |
| Clarity | Can they explain what this is, who it is for, the outcome, why it matters, and the next step? | Actual hero wording plus a headings-only skim |
| Completeness | Can they make the intended decision or complete the task without a material information gap? | Applicable scope, price approach, process, timing, requirements, fit, revisions, support, and terms |
| Proof | Are consequential claims supported by evidence that establishes those specific claims? | Claim/evidence pairings and sources; absent evidence limits claims and should be disclosed, not fabricated |
| Low friction | Is the next step understandable, proportionate, and easy to find? | CTA labels, expected commitment, form requirements, practical details, and confirmation expectations |

For each applicable dimension record **met**, **needs work**, or **not assessable**, plus the passage or missing fact supporting the judgment. When only copy is available, assess wording and proposed placement; leave visual scanning, actual clicks, mobile layout, and real completion behavior unverified.

Finally, trace the page through this visitor progression:

**"Yes, this is for me -> They understand my problem -> I understand their solution -> I trust it -> I know what to do next."**

Identify where the chain breaks and repair that point. Treat it as a comprehension check, not a requirement to add five sections, force an emotional narrative, or manufacture social proof.
