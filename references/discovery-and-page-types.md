# Discovery prompts and page-specific decisions

Use this bank selectively. Ask only unanswered questions that materially affect the requested page. Explain what is missing rather than repeating a generic questionnaire. Let the user answer in bullets and mark unknowns. Gather anonymized customer patterns, not private records.

## First discovery round

If no brief exists, start with these six grouped prompts:

1. Which page are we creating or improving, and what does your business sell or provide? Share existing copy or a link if useful.
2. Who is the main visitor, and what problem or situation makes them need this now?
3. Where will they arrive from, and what question should this page answer first?
4. What exactly are you offering, and what useful result can the customer reasonably expect?
5. What is the main action they should take, and what happens immediately afterward?
6. What makes them hesitate or choose an alternative, and what truthful evidence or business detail would help them decide?

Do not ask this round verbatim when the user has already supplied answers.

## Targeted follow-ups

| Gap | Useful questions |
|---|---|
| Audience | Who is the priority if several groups visit? Who pays versus uses it? Who is a poor fit? What do visitors already understand? |
| Offer | What is included and excluded? How is it delivered? What are prerequisites, timelines, availability, or limits? What revisions and ongoing support are included, if relevant? |
| Intent | What search query, ad promise, referring page, or task brought them? Do they want to learn, compare, buy, book, verify, or contact? Are they finding a nearby provider? What answer would let them move forward fastest? |
| Benefits | What changes in the customer's day or business? Which benefit matters most? Compared with doing nothing or the usual alternative, what is factually different? |
| Barriers | What questions appear in sales or support? Is the barrier cost, trust, complexity, time, fit, switching, or risk? Which conditions should visitors learn early? |
| Proof | What verified case studies, demonstrations, credentials, product details, or approved testimonials exist? What exactly does each support? If none exist, which claims should we omit or soften? |
| Action | Does the button open a form, pricing page, checkout, calendar, call, or another resource? What commitment is involved? What details are genuinely needed? What happens after submission? |
| Voice | What words do customers use for the problem? What language and tone should we use? Are there examples you like or phrases to avoid? |
| Context | Is this search traffic, paid traffic, email, referral, or direct? What promise must match? Any existing navigation, mobile constraints, or page relationships? |
| Boundaries | Required wording, claim restrictions, accessibility needs, geography, content length, or brand rules? What observable visitor outcome means the page worked? |

## Adapt to the page

| Page | Additional facts to establish | Prioritize in content |
|---|---|---|
| Homepage | Business category, primary audience, main offerings, priority visitor routes | Business clarity first; a dominant next action with obvious routes where multiple needs genuinely exist |
| Campaign landing page | Exact ad/email promise, audience temperature, offer terms, campaign action | Message match, focused offer, immediate relevance, decision proof, clear action |
| Service page | Scope, deliverables, process, eligibility, location, pricing or quote approach | Outcome, fit, what is included, credible process, cost expectations, enquiry/booking path |
| Product or SaaS page | Use case, capabilities, requirements, integrations, trial/demo terms | Practical outcomes supported by features, fit, demonstration, evaluation or purchase step |
| Ecommerce product page | Variants, accurate price, stock source, shipping, returns, specifications | Purchase-critical details close to action, tangible benefits, fit/size, delivery and return confidence |
| Pricing page | Billing unit and period, currencies, plan differences, limits, fees, renewal/cancellation terms | Understandable total commitment, plan fit, meaningful comparison, straightforward selection |
| Local business/location page | Actual location or service area, services available there, hours, contact/booking route | Local relevance, availability, practical visit/contact details, genuine local evidence; no invented branches |
| Informational page | Exact question, reader knowledge, necessary depth, authoritative facts | Direct answer early, useful explanation, task completion, optional relevant next step |
| About page | Origin, expertise, people, practices, evidence relevant to buyer trust | Why the business can serve this customer, credible human detail, useful onward route |
| Comparison page | Alternatives, decision criteria, verified capabilities, audience tradeoffs | Fair differences, fit and limitations, evidence, next evaluation step; no invented competitor weaknesses |
| Contact/booking page | Available channels, required fields, eligibility, hours, actual response/booking process | Easy completion, accurate expectations, essential practical information |

## Handling gaps without inventing answers

- Broad audience: offer a few plausible audience choices and ask which takes priority; do not silently pick one.
- Unknown traffic: document that it is unknown, then establish the visitor task from the business and audience. Do not invent keyword research.
- No testimonials: use verified process, scope, demonstrations, or qualifications if available; otherwise write without social proof.
- Unclear pricing: establish whether pricing is public, variable, or quote-based and how customers get an accurate amount. Do not obscure price information already available.
- No conversion process: ask the user to choose the intended next step and define what follows before writing CTA promises.
- Readiness at 80/100: ask the highest-value remaining question to reach 85, while still requiring the four core areas to be fully resolved.
- High score but ambiguous audience or action: remain in discovery; numeric completeness never bypasses the core requirements.
- Existing detailed brief: reuse it, score it, and ask only questions that address material gaps. Do not force a fresh interview.
- Several pages: share business facts, but assess page-specific audience, intent, and action independently. Draft ready pages while gathering missing information for others.
