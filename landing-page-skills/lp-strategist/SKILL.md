# Landing page strategy

1. Map traffic to page action, follow-up, and business outcome. Check the available baseline, offer economics, delivery or follow-up capacity, and whether changing the page addresses the likely bottleneck. Read client materials, ads or outreach, sales questions, search terms, and existing analytics where available. Record evidence by source. Label competitor observations, buyer hypotheses, and client-confirmed facts distinctly.
2. Pick the priority buyer and buying situation, desired outcome, and alternatives. Map the promise visitors saw before arrival to the first decision the page must support. When sources imply different intents, decide which one this page serves or propose separate variants.
3. Specify the offer, useful differentiator, top objection, and proof to address it. Track each material claim with source, approval status, and scope. Flag gaps that block publication. Continue with credible supported facts and explicit assumptions in working drafts.
4. Choose one primary action and define what happens immediately afterwards. Set the business outcome and the earliest trustworthy success signal. For lead generation, agree on the receiving destination, receiving owner, and what makes a lead qualified. An approved existing sales inbox with a reliable receiving record can serve as the destination when a CRM is undecided. Ask tracking to define event and attribution mechanics.
5. Define the minimum information required for the next step. Give each proposed form field a purpose tied to follow-up or qualification. Confirm availability, pricing, and material promises when relevant.
6. Deliver the conversion brief, updating an existing shared brief where available. Include audience, traffic message, offer, positioning, objections, approved evidence, CTA, receiving owner, success definition, constraints, and unresolved decisions. Make the reasoning behind the major choices visible.

For a course enquiry, the initial page outcome is an accepted enquiry. Evaluate quality once sales applies a documented criterion. Treat enrollment as measurable only when a downstream record can be connected. Avoid assumptions about placement, salaries, credentials, dates, or prerequisites in publishable claims.

## Apply the marketing context

Reuse the project's existing product or marketing brief and prior campaign learnings before asking for inputs. Mark angles as supported, proposed, or previously tested. Keep the traffic promise, priority objection, defensible claim, and conversion goal together so copy, tracking, and CRO use the same decision. If evidence suggests traffic fit or sales follow-up is the main problem, identify the observation that would distinguish it from a page problem.
