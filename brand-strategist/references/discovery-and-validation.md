# Discovery and Validation

## Discovery questions

Ask only unanswered questions that materially affect the decision. Select up to five to begin:

1. What specific business outcome should this strategy support, and what is currently preventing it?
2. Who most recently chose your business, what triggered their search, and what alternative did they consider?
3. What can your business consistently deliver today, and what proof supports that?
4. What do customers misunderstand, question, praise, or complain about?
5. What existing recognition, resources, deadlines, and operational constraints must the work respect?

Separate founder beliefs from customer accounts. Request examples over adjectives.

## Customer interview prompts

Ask about recent real events before hypothetical preferences:

- Tell me about the last time you needed this kind of solution.
- What happened that made you start looking?
- What did you try before that? What was insufficient?
- Which alternatives did you seriously consider?
- What almost stopped you from choosing this business?
- What evidence helped you feel confident?
- What happened after purchase? What met or missed expectations?
- How would you explain this business to someone in the same situation?

Avoid leading questions, invented quotes, pressure to agree, and treating purchase intentions as actual purchases. Record sample limitations and relevant differences between buyers, non-buyers, and lost customers. Do not contact participants without authorization.

## Validation design

| Assumption | Relevant participants | Task/stimulus | Observation | Decision rule and basis | Action if weak | Owner role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Choose tests suited to the uncertainty:

- Comprehension: show a short message, remove it, then ask what is offered, for whom, and what happens next.
- Relevance: connect the proposition to an actual recent buying situation; investigate trade-offs and priority.
- Credibility: ask what feels doubtful and what evidence would resolve it.
- Preference: compare realistic alternatives with comparable information and ask for reasoning.
- Recognition: test delayed recall or recognition of distinctive elements without displaying the name.
- Experience: compare the actual journey with the intended promise and identify contradictory moments.

Set decision rules before seeing results. Do not invent universal pass rates or statistical certainty from small qualitative samples. Record sample, recruitment method, stimulus, date, result, limitations, and resulting revisions. Label unperformed tests as planned.

## Example prompts

- Use $brand-strategist to audit our website and sales materials. Identify the three most important brand problems and recommend the smallest useful repairs.
- Use $brand-strategist to develop positioning for our business. Compare strategic routes, recommend one, and show what proof and delivery capabilities it requires.
- Use $brand-strategist to create an identity direction brief using our approved positioning. Preserve recognized assets and work within one designer's capacity.
- Use $brand-strategist to assess whether we need a rebrand. Compare retaining, refreshing, repositioning, and replacing the identity before recommending changes.
